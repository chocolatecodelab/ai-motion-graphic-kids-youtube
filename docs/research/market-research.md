# Market Research — Kids Educational Animation (ages 8–12, global, English)

Status: Phase 0 draft · Researched 2026-11-04 · Owner review required

## How to read this document

Every claim is tagged:

- **[E] Evidence**: a source exists. Primary or official sources are preferred.
- **[S] Secondary**: a third-party aggregator or blog. Use it directionally and re-verify before any business decision.
- **[A] Assumption**: our inference. It must be validated with channel analytics once videos are live.

Limitation: in this research environment, direct page fetches to vendor and Google domains were blocked by the network policy. Findings come from web-search summaries of those pages. Everything marked [E] should be re-read at the source before it is relied on for spend or compliance.

**Implications [A]:**
- 8–12 is the transition age. They are old enough for 6–10 minute narrative explainers (Kurzgesagt-lite) and young enough that content is legally child-directed (see §4).
- Parents and teachers are a real secondary audience and decide whether a channel is "allowed". Trust signals matter: sources in the description, no clickbait.

## 3. Interest areas and evergreen demand

Topic list lineup from kid-oriented channels and parent guides [S]: animals and nature, the human body, space, how things work, history (ancient civilisations), weird facts and mysteries. This matches our 5 pillars.

Evergreen candidates [A] (to be validated with keyword tools and analytics):
- **"Why…" questions**: why is the sky blue, why do cats purr, why do we dream.
- **"How does X work"**: airplanes, the heart, volcanoes, toilets, the internet.
- **"What if…"**: what if the Moon disappeared. These are high curiosity but need careful framing so they don't frighten.
- **Superlatives**: the biggest animal ever, the deepest ocean point.
- **Curriculum-adjacent topics** (water cycle, photosynthesis, simple machines, solar system). These get search plus teacher demand all year, with peaks during the school term.

**Strategic implications [A]. These are the most important findings in this document.**

1. **Our content is legally child-directed (under 13).** The "8–12" target means MFK = true. This is not optional and is not a growth lever. Consequences:
   - lower ad RPM;
   - no comments, so no community feedback loop;
   - no end screens or bell;
   - discovery depends on search, suggested videos, YouTube Kids and playlists.
   The analytics loop must therefore lean on retention and CTR, not comments.
2. **"AI kids content" is under heavy public and regulatory scrutiny in 2026.** A daily AI factory is exactly the profile YouTube is policing. We differentiate on:
   - verified facts with visible sources;
   - a consistent hand-approved character (no per-frame generative drift, no warped faces);
   - human approval of every episode until trust is earned;
   - honest AI disclosure.
   Quality beats volume. Daily cadence must never come at the expense of the quality bar.
3. **Monetisation should be diversified from day one [A].** Options:
   - sponsorship from education brands (COPPA-compliant, non-manipulative);
   - licensing to schools and edtech platforms;
   - merch only much later;
   - YouTube Kids inclusion.
   Budget assumptions must not depend on high MFK RPM.

## 5. Long-form vs Shorts

| Finding | Tag |
|---|---|
| Shorts can be MFK too, and MFK Shorts lose the same features. | [A] (verify in YouTube Help) |
| Long-form 5–8 min gives room for the Question→Adventure→Discovery arc. Typical competitor lengths: Crash Course Kids 3–6 min, Free School 5–15, Kurzgesagt 8–15, Homeschool Pop 5–20. | [S] |
| Shorts are useful as discovery trailers, built from the "Twist" fact plus the hook, and cheap to derive from existing renders. | [A] |

Recommendation: long-form is the product. Shorts are derived, 1–2 per episode, generated from the existing scene renders at near-zero marginal cost (Phase 6+).
