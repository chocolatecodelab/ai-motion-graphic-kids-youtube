# Scene rules
- One storyboard scene per script scene, same keys (S01, S02, ...) and beats, in order.
- `line_ids` lists every line of that scene, in order. Every script line appears exactly once in the whole storyboard.
- `duration_s` covers the scene's spoken lines (sum of their estimates) plus breathing room for action and transitions, usually 0.5–2.5 s extra. Total target: {min_s}–{max_s} s.
- `background.theme`: the environment the renderer draws. Pick the closest one: attic, workbench, sunbeam, sky, air, sunset, mars, space, studio. Match the scene's location: the grandparent's attic is `attic`, riding a beam of light is `sunbeam`, among air molecules is `air`.
- `background.asset_request`: a short description of that place as a flat vector scene in the brand palette (Explorer Blue #243B75, Discovery Yellow #F8C84A, Adventure Orange #F47C45, Nature Teal #55BBA5, Cloud Cream #F4F0E8). No text in backgrounds.
- `transition_out`: `compass_portal` when the duo travels somewhere new, `whip` or `zoom_through` for energetic jumps, otherwise `cut` or a short `wipe`.
- `educational_purpose`: what the viewer should understand by the end of the scene.

# Direct it like an animated adventure film, not a slideshow
Each scene has 1–4 `shots`. A new shot starts on a line (`starts_at_line`); the first shot has `starts_at_line: null`. Cut to a new shot when the focus, emotion or idea changes.

**Shot types:**
- `extreme_wide`: establishes a place, characters small;
- `wide`: full bodies and environment;
- `medium`: waist up, conversation;
- `close_up`: face, reactions and emotion;
- `extreme_close_up`: eyes, the compass, a tiny detail;
- `over_shoulder`: looking past one character at the other or at a discovery;
- `pov`: what a character sees;
- `insert`: a cutaway to an object or diagram.

**Angles:**
- `eye_level`: neutral;
- `low`: looking up, for heroic, awe or "whoa" moments;
- `high`: looking down, for small, overwhelmed or curious moments;
- `birds_eye`: top-down, for maps and scale;
- `tilted`: off-balance, for comedy or surprise.

**Camera moves:** `zoom_in`/`dolly_in` toward a discovery, `zoom_out`/`dolly_out` to reveal scale, `pan_*`/`truck_*` to follow travel, `follow` to track a moving character, `tilt_up`/`crane_up` to reveal the sky, `whip_pan` for fast energy, `shake` for an impact or bump, `orbit` around a hologram.

**Blocking** (per shot, per on-screen character):
- `x` 0..1 is the horizontal position and `y` 0..1 the ground line, where 1 is the bottom of the frame.
- `scale` 1 = medium-shot size (about 2.5 for a close-up, 0.4 for an extreme wide).
- `depth`: foreground, midground or background. `facing`: left, right or camera.
- Give characters `motion` (walk, run, fly, float, jump, shrink, grow, spin, bounce, zip) and a `path` of up to 4 waypoints timed to lines (`at_line`), plus `enter`/`exit` (from_left, from_right, from_top, from_bottom, pop, portal) so they move through the space.
- Needle is a tiny flying spark: it zips around Kai, lands on a shoulder, flies into the scene, peeks from behind things. It is NOT a second character standing on the right.

**Variety rules (checked by code):**
- Never three shots in a row with the same shot type.
- At least 4 different shot types and 3 different angles in the episode.
- At least 60% of shots have camera or character movement, and every adventure scene has movement.
- Do NOT park Kai on the left and Needle on the right shot after shot: vary positions, depth and scale.
- Whoever speaks must be in that shot's `blocking`, unless the shot is an `insert`, `pov` or `extreme_wide`, or its focus is `graphic`/`background` (voice-over).

# Motion graphics
- Use components to make the science visible exactly when it is said (`at_line`). Prefer one clear graphic over several busy ones. Props must be simple values that match the component's purpose. A shot whose focus is `graphic` frames the graphic large.
- **Never invent numbers.** A graphic may show a number only if a verified claim states it; list that claim in `claim_ids`. For a qualitative comparison ("less violet", "much smaller"), set `illustrative: true`: the renderer then draws relative shapes without numbers, axes or units. Relative sizes must still point the right way and not understate a huge difference.
