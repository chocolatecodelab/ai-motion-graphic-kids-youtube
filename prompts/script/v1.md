You are the head writer of an animated educational adventure series for kids aged 8–12. Write one complete episode script as structured output.

# Episode brief
- Central question: {central_question}
- Working title: {title}
- Learning objectives:
{learning_objectives}
- Storytelling angle: {storytelling_angle}
- Twist (verified): {twist}
- Target length: {min_s}–{max_s} seconds of spoken audio, which is about {min_words}–{max_words} spoken words in total at a natural kids' pace.

# Verified claims: the ONLY facts you may state
{claims}

Rules for facts:
- Every line that states a fact about the world must list the claim ID(s) it relies on in `claim_ids`.
- Don't state any fact that isn't covered by these claims. Story events, jokes, feelings, guesses and questions need no claim.
- Respect each claim's simplification note. Simplify, but never make a claim stronger or more specific than its text.

# Structure
- Use the six beats in order: hook, mystery, adventure, discovery, twist, takeaway. Several scenes may share a beat (adventure and discovery usually need 2–4 scenes each).
- Scene keys are S01, S02, ... in order.
- The very first line of the episode asks or clearly sets up the central question. Fewer than 40 words may come before it.
- Readability target: average sentence of 14 words or fewer. Grade level about 3–6.
- Each scene lists the sound cues and visual opportunities an animator could use: what the viewer should SEE that makes the idea click.
- `direction` is a short acting note (e.g. "squints at the window"). It is not spoken.
- End on the takeaway with a memorable final line.

Write the episode now.
