# Brand and storytelling guide

## The channel
An animated educational adventure channel for curious kids aged 8–12, watched worldwide in English.
- Concept: "Every Question Opens a New Adventure."
- Tagline: "Explore. Wonder. Discover."
- Promise: every episode is a real discovery inside an entertaining adventure. Roughly half entertainment, half learning, and never at the cost of accuracy.
- Personality: curious, adventurous, playful, smart, imaginative, friendly, trustworthy.

## Story formula: QUESTION → ADVENTURE → DISCOVERY
Every episode answers one central question in six beats:
1. **Hook**: the central question, asked within the first 15 seconds, in a way that makes a kid want the answer.
2. **Mystery**: a relatable situation. Why is this puzzling? What would a kid guess first?
3. **Adventure**: the characters physically go somewhere (shrink, zoom, portal, time-jump) to investigate. The adventure is how the explanation is shown, not decoration.
4. **Discovery**: the core idea, explained with one clear visual analogy at a time.
5. **Twist**: one surprising, verified fact that reframes what they learned.
6. **Takeaway**: a short recap a kid could repeat to a friend, and a memorable ending.

## Writing style
- Natural spoken English for 8–12-year-olds anywhere in the world. Short sentences. Concrete words. No slang that only works in one country.
- Explain one idea at a time. Use an analogy only if it is accurate enough not to mislead.
- Humor comes from character and situation, not from talking down to the audience.
- The characters discover answers *with* the viewer. They never lecture.
- Name a scientific term only after the idea is clear, and then once ("Scientists call this...").

## Never
- Generic AI phrasing or YouTube filler: "Let's dive in", "In today's video", "Hey guys", "Don't forget to like and subscribe", "Buckle up", "Without further ado", "Ever wondered...?" as the first words.
- Asking viewers to comment, share personal information, or go to other websites.
- False suspense, repeated explanations, or padding.
- Facts that are not in the verified claim list.
